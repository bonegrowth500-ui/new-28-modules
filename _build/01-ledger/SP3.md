## Chunk-level insights

- **Biggest deepening: the video craft is sales machinery that is only used on video.** The question chain, the three reframe methods, credibility moments and the "conversion-load-bearing" hidden-variable reframe map directly onto sales pages, offer videos, email sequences and calls. Reuse them without re-teaching them: order assets by the buyer's questions, map each objection to a reframe, and add "who this is not for" sections and scope limits (SP3-053, 059, 064, 105, 112, 369).
- **Put a price on each video.** The chunk says a missing layer shows up "in its retention graph or its conversion" but never measures conversion. Its autopsy has causes and fixes but no thresholds ("partly normal," "large," "mostly normal"). Add ranges for intro retention and average percentage viewed by length and traffic source, plus per-video first-step conversions, CTA-placement tests and data-led clip selection. The worked graph (75% at 0:30, 45% at 1:30) supplies anchor points (SP3-177, 182, 187, 190, 366).
- **Production economics are implied but never built.** "Reserve the expensive format for the assets that earn back the cost over years," "a four-hour edit into ninety minutes" and "a hundred-dollar mic, free editing software" add up to a cost-per-video and payback model for a solo operator with freelancers. Allowed spend per video = expected lifetime views × first-step conversion × customer value × margin (SP3-237, 246, 267, 373).
- **The free/paid line on the diagnostic is the offer decision this chunk forces.** The worked video gives the self-assessment away as its payload and sends viewers to a free community for help running it; the other document trades an "Assessment Guide" for an email. With no free community, decide what content gives away (the self-check), what is sold (the expert assessment), how the close scripts change, and which videos may carry a direct paid CTA (SP3-026, 149, 153, 154, 370).
- **Face-first flips half of Part 6, but keep its market read.** Graduation, voiceover and the faceless format stack are OUT. The fatigue with "faces selling faces" becomes a face-on-camera brand's core positioning problem from day one: authority on method and judgment, never on the operator's own appearance; a decision on whether his own face or results ever count as proof; a stance on appearance-judgment comments. Format mix, production order (lighting, camera and framing now matter), scripting mode and delivery register all need fresh decisions (SP3-005, 198, 233, 251, 316, 326, 327).
- **Offer guarantees vs the ban on guaranteed claims.** The claim hierarchy bans guaranteed claims outright, yet offer guarantees are in scope. Separate outcome guarantees (never) from risk-reversal guarantees (refund, conditional, action-based), and link them to the timeline objection (SP3-165, 369).
- **In-scope legal and platform gaps, all to be kept brief.** Typicality disclosure for results testimonials; the net-impression principle for images; the FTC's 2024 rule on fake and AI-generated testimonials; YouTube's disclosure for realistic synthetic content; written releases, with parental consent for clients under 18; scope of practice for breathing or bite issues; copyright and licensing for clips, stock, music beds and web photos (SP3-074, 111, 112, 287, 340, 345, 375).
- **Live selling is where the overclaim traps bite.** In video, the script catches excitement drift, overgeneralization, causal shortcuts and scope creep. Calls, DMs, lives and podcast guesting have no script, so they need an answer bank, money-moment scripting and a same-person test between the content persona and the closer (SP3-167, 372, 376).
- **Personal-brand economics.** "People pay a premium to work with someone they feel they know" is pricing power, while "more defensible and portable" hides key-person and capacity risk. The operator layer should quantify both and settle personal name vs method brand (SP3-300, 302; SP2-114 to SP2-117).
- **Early-stage proof is an open question.** The model hook's proof is "in my experience," and Part 7 promises credibility "without your own face or your own results." For a stage-agnostic, face-first blueprint, the question is honest proof before client results exist (SP3-365, 371).
- **Contradictions with the other document to decide fresh.** Energy: "bring 20% more energy," "speak slightly louder" and "speed up for excitement" vs calm restraint that never accelerates toward a payoff. Production: lighting as "the most important element" and a phone-mic floor vs audio first with a dedicated mic. Interrupts: "every few minutes" vs 40–90 s. Scripting: "don't memorize word-for-word" vs full script. Structure: numbered "Mistake #1" sections and chapters vs question chains and curiosity-named chapters. Contrarian posture: enemy framing and "Everything You Know… is Wrong" titles vs steelman-first. Real faces: celebrity rating and arrows on features vs the dignity ban. Intensity: PAS agitation vs stakes without shame. Endings: like, comment, subscribe vs one matched next step (SP3-061, 071, 077, 085, 097, 100, 115, 161, 208, 251, 286).
- **Internal contradictions not to inherit.** A three-second logo sting on the open vs the five-second click-confirm. 40–90 s visual interrupts (b-roll included) vs "decorative b-roll… signals 'content farm.'"
